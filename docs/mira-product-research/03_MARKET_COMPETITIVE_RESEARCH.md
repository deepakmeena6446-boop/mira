# 03 — Market and competitive research

_Desk research as of 2 October 2026. This is a product strategy audit, not a usability test or a claim that every feature works equally well in every city. Product facts below come from current first-party product/help pages; review excerpts and research findings are labelled separately. Frequency, failure-mode and strategic conclusions are **inferences** unless explicitly evidenced._

## Executive judgment

Mira competes with a **stack**, not a single women’s safety app: a map to choose a route and inspect places; a messenger or location-sharing app to tell someone where she is; a ride or transit app to execute a journey; a community or search product to ask what an area is like; an AI assistant to synthesize general advice; and emergency services when something happens. Google Maps already shows routes, places, opening hours, reviews, live busyness and location sharing. Ask Maps now accepts conversational place questions in the US and India; Gemini can combine Maps, hotels and flights. A generic “ask about safety” plus a pin map will not hold a defensible position. [Google Maps Help](https://support.google.com/maps/answer/144349), [Maps busyness explanation](https://support.google.com/maps/answer/11323117), [Ask Maps announcement](https://blog.google/products-and-platforms/products/maps/ask-maps-immersive-navigation/), [Gemini travel planning](https://blog.google/products-and-platforms/products/gemini/how-gemini-plans-trips/).

The most promising gap is **decision support at the moment of movement**: *For this person’s intended activity, route, mode and time, what should she know; what workable options does she have; and what changes if she becomes uncomfortable?* Competitors provide strong components, but their data and workflows remain split. This is a hypothesis for user validation, not a claim of proven market demand.

## What competitors solve well

1. **They attach safety to a task already happening.** Uber and Lyft show trip safety controls inside the ride. Nike and Strava add sharing inside a run. Citymapper gives the next transit action while the journey unfolds. This is a retention lesson: Mira should become useful *before and during movement*, with escalation available but not the only reason to open it. [Uber](https://www.uber.com/us/en/safety/our-commitment/), [Lyft](https://help.lyft.com/hc/en/all/articles/360051084234-Sharing-your-ride-location-with-friends-and-family), [Nike](https://about.nike.com/en/newsroom/releases/nike-run-club-app-new-features), [Citymapper GO](https://citymapper.com/news/517/go-your-personal-trip-assistant).
2. **Data provenance changes the promise.** Citizen’s emergency dispatch monitoring can support a claim about *reported ongoing incidents*, not general neighborhood safety. Safetipin’s trained and crowdsourced audits support *public-space condition* claims. Strava can support *where its athletes were active*, not crowd density or harassment risk. Google’s busyness is aggregated from opted-in users and deliberately withheld when data is insufficient; even this large network does not show exactly where people are. Mira must label signals this carefully. [Citizen criteria](https://support.citizen.com/hc/en-us/articles/115000603373-What-is-Citizen-s-criteria-for-reporting-incidents), [Safetipin methodology](https://safetipin.com/methodology/), [Strava](https://press.strava.com/articles/strava-expands-mapping-tools-with-night-and-weekly-heatmaps), [Google busyness](https://support.google.com/maps/answer/11323117).
3. **Contribution works when it is low effort and immediately useful.** Waze asks a passing driver to confirm whether a road event is still there, and suppresses distracting alerts. This is a better model for rapidly changing observations than a permanent public accusation pin. [Waze confirmation](https://support.google.com/waze/answer/12237550), [Waze alert prioritization](https://support.google.com/waze/answer/13786535).
4. **Human operations still matter.** Noonlight’s dispatchers and Citizen’s editors give those products capabilities a thin app cannot safely claim. Safetipin’s trained coders and institutional audits likewise show that reliable local data requires operations, not only a crowdsourced form. [Noonlight process](https://help.noonlight.com/en/articles/2060965-how-does-noonlight-work), [Citizen process](https://support.citizen.com/hc/en-us/articles/115000278894-How-does-Citizen-work), [Safetipin methodology](https://safetipin.com/methodology/).

## Where the market fails, with evidence strength

### Reliability is product value, not a technical detail

The bSafe Play listing describes a broad SOS/guardian feature set, but its visible rating was **2.0/5 across roughly 8.9K reviews** at audit time. Highlighted reviewers report crashes, inaccurate tracking and a paywall around essential features. These are individual reports and a store aggregate, not an independently measured failure rate. They show why a safety promise can collapse if basic flows fail or if users discover restrictions after setup. [bSafe Play listing and reviews](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.bipper.app.bsafe). Strava explicitly notes Beacon requires cellular service, a manually sent link, and does not send stopped-activity alerts; Garmin warns LiveTrack incident detection is not a primary emergency method. [Strava Beacon](https://support.strava.com/en-us/articles/15401829-strava-beacon), [Garmin LiveTrack](https://support.garmin.com/en-IN/?faq=HbqxxbiBGA3mDhlLX4GUw8&productID=125677&tab=).

### More alerts can make people less capable

A peer-reviewed CHI 2023 case study of Citizen reports participants found frequent alerts stressful or anxiety-inducing, despite valuing awareness. The authors characterize a pattern where the product can both create and relieve anxiety. This is stronger evidence than app reviews, though it is a case study of Citizen rather than all safety tools. Citizen App Store reviewers separately complain about far-away or stale incidents, false reports, harsh comments and subscription prompts. Reviews are selected complaints, not representative prevalence. [CHI paper](https://faculty.washington.edu/alexisr/SafetyApps.pdf), [Citizen App Store reviews](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/citizen-safety-live-video/id1039889567). Mira should optimize for **relevant, action-changing information**, not a larger alert/feed inventory.

### A mapped “safe place” is not a usable option by default

WalkSafe combines UK journey sharing and a national safe-space map; Safetipin combines route and support centers. They establish user interest in place-based assistance, but their public descriptions alone do not prove a place is open, staffed, accessible, willing to help or reachable by an appropriate route at the exact time of need. That gap is an **inference from the data fields these products publicly describe**, and should be tested in product walkthroughs. [WalkSafe](https://walksafe.io/), [Safetipin](https://safetipin.com/my-safetipin-app/). Mira should use a higher standard before calling a location a “help point” or “safe space.”

### Global branding often hides city-level density limits

Citizen’s App Store listing says free alerts are strongest in named US/Canada metros. Citymapper describes 430 cities and transit-agency dependencies. Safetipin’s field work and institutional audits are city-specific. This argues for a globally available **baseline** of official travel advice, map/place context and clearly bounded guidance, with local community/route intelligence turned on only where coverage passes thresholds. [Citizen listing](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/citizen-safety-live-video/id1039889567), [Citymapper](https://citymapper.com/news/2826/2025-thats-a-wrap), [Safetipin projects](https://safetyscore.safetipin.com/projects).

### Broad AI has moved into local discovery

In 2026, Google launched conversational Ask Maps in the US and India, and Gemini travel planning uses live Maps/Hotels/Flights information. ChatGPT search can use optional device location for nearby results. Therefore “conversational local travel advice” is already a commodity. Mira’s proposed edge cannot be merely an AI voice on top of generic map search; it needs evidence about the actual movement context that those systems do not hold, together with checks, uncertainty and appropriate actions. [Ask Maps](https://blog.google/products-and-platforms/products/maps/ask-maps-immersive-navigation/), [Gemini travel planning](https://blog.google/products-and-platforms/products/gemini/how-gemini-plans-trips/), [ChatGPT search](https://help.openai.com/en/articles/9237897-searching-the-web-with-chatgpt).

## Strategic takeaways for Mira

**Borrow:** (1) activity-native entry from ride and run apps; (2) Citymapper’s concise, timely step support; (3) Waze’s expiring observations and local confirmation; (4) Safetipin’s explicit environmental dimensions; (5) official advisories’ provenance; (6) emergency services’ clear escalation boundary.

**Avoid:** (1) a generic public report feed; (2) permanent or precise “safe” scores; (3) forcing continuous location sharing; (4) charging to reveal essential safety information after a user opens an alert; (5) treating a route’s popularity or area busyness as proof of safety; (6) claiming human response or emergency dispatch without the operations and partnerships to provide it.

**Candidate position to validate:** Mira becomes a **contextual movement companion**: the user states an activity and destination or route, Mira explains the few signals that affect that specific decision, offers workable options and handoffs to the tools that execute them, and helps the user adapt if circumstances change. Its distinct layer would be structured, time- and mode-aware observations about movement conditions and verified support options, with coverage shown honestly. This is a proposed synthesis, not an established moat.

### The replacement test

| Proposed capability | ChatGPT + Google Maps today | Mira’s threshold to matter |
|---|---|---|
| General destination tips, basic neighborhood description, nearby police/hospital, walking directions | Largely available | **Do not build as a differentiator.** Offer only as baseline or handoff. |
| “I run at 5 AM: compare these two loops” | Can combine sunrise, route length and public place data, but lacks validated, time-specific observations of each segment | Need fresh segment-level conditions, provenance, coverage/unknowns and a useful alternative. |
| “I land late: how do I reach this hotel?” | Can outline official transport, ride-hail, hotel pickup and route | Need current arrival context, terminal/exit and hotel handoff details, local modes, verified pickup/access information and explicit uncertainty. |
| “I feel followed; where can I go right now?” | Can suggest generic advice and nearby open places | Need situation-appropriate, verified, open, staffed and reachable options; a direct action; and emergency escalation. Do not promise this without operational validation. |
| Share my live position or call emergency services | Existing OS, chat and platform tools do this well | Integrate and simplify handoff; duplicating alone is weak differentiation. |

## Source and interpretation cautions

- Product pages describe vendor intent and current advertised availability. They do not establish outcome effectiveness.
- Store reviews are firsthand accounts, but selected and unrepresentative; use them to identify failure modes for testing, not to estimate incidence.
- The Citizen CHI study is directly relevant to fear and alert design, but it studies one product in a particular context.
- Country/city coverage, pricing and app behavior change quickly. Recheck provider terms and live UX before a build decision or partnership.
