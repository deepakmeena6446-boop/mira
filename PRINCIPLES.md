# MIRA product principles

MIRA helps a woman understand the way before she goes, keeps the people she chooses with her until she arrives, and puts help one tap away. These principles are the test every change must pass: code, copy, data and design. A pull request that can't say which principle it serves, and whether it breaks any, isn't ready.

## The principles

1. **Walk with her, don't warn her.** Calm, factual, non-fear language. No red maps, no danger scores, and never "safe" or "unsafe" for a place, route or person.

2. **Location only while she chooses, gone when she's done.** Live location exists only during a trip she started, only for people she picked, and is deleted at the end. No location history, no passive tracking.

3. **About streets, never about people.** Community signals describe infrastructure and conditions (light, footpaths, open places), never individuals, groups or who lives somewhere.

4. **Many voices, shown with doubt.** No single person's input is ever shown. Everything public says where it came from, roughly when, and what isn't known.

5. **Every safety promise must be true when it matters.** MIRA never says a message went out, a contact is watching or someone will be told unless that is actually the case. When something fails, she is told.

6. **Contributing takes seconds.** A tap beats a form. If a contribution takes minutes, redesign it.

7. **She decides; MIRA proposes.** Nothing is shared, sent or started without her tap. MIRA and its assistant, Mira, are not an emergency service and never pretend to be.

8. **Context, not verdicts.** MIRA shows sources, recency, confidence and unknowns. It never says safe or unsafe. A Women's Mobility / Safety Index may come later, but only if rigorous research and a transparent methodology support it.

9. **Standalone value, honest coverage.** MIRA is useful to one person with no community participation. How much context it can give depends on the data available in each place. When reliable information isn't available, MIRA says "not known" rather than estimating or inventing an answer. Community makes it better; it is never the reason it works.

10. **Evidence decides truth; AI decides relevance.** Whether a safety-related claim is true, and how confident MIRA is, comes from evidence: source provenance, deterministic rules, corroboration, recency, contradiction and moderation where needed. AI may decide what is relevant, prioritise, personalise and suggest actions. It never decides on its own whether a safety claim is true, and its output is never evidence.

11. **Old is not current.** Every item has a validity window. Expired items disappear; dated items show their date.

12. **Incidents are evidence inputs, not the product UI.** A verified transport disruption, official warning, road closure or serious nearby event may appear as short guidance only if it is geographically and temporally relevant, from a confident source, corroborated where appropriate, and something she can act on. No doomscroll feeds, raw crime maps, sensational alerts or neighbourhood stigma.

13. **Global by profile, local by data.** The core is country-neutral. Each country is a cited, reviewed profile (emergency numbers, helplines, and so on). India is the first profile, not the default worldview.

## What MIRA will not build

This list records decisions made against the principles. Proposals for anything on it will be closed with a link here.

- Simplistic neighbourhood safety scores.
- Unexplained 0–100 safety ratings.
- Rankings driven by raw community sentiment.
- Ratings of people or of demographic groups.
- Fear-based red/green maps, including crime heatmaps.
- Crime-news feeds.
- People-reporting: reports about individuals, what they look like, or where they live.
- Passive or background tracking, or any location history.
- AI-asserted safety facts: statements about the world that come from a model rather than from evidence.

**Not on this list:** a research-backed Women's Mobility / Safety Index. It is a possible long-term research output, to be considered only with rigorous, transparent, published methodology (see principle 8). It is not planned work.

## Using the principles

- Every pull request answers two questions: *Which principle does this serve?* and *Does it collect, store, show or send any new data?*
- Roadmap decisions are made in writing by maintainers, against these principles and evidence from real users (interviews, support messages, usage counts). Votes and reactions show interest; they don't decide.
- Changes to this file are a protected area (see [CONTRIBUTING.md](CONTRIBUTING.md)).
