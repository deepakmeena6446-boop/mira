# Mira market and open-source research

*Research date: 30 September 2026. Desk research from current official product/help pages, app-store listings, project documentation and source repositories. This is a pattern study, not a claim to have completed authenticated competitor app flows. Observations are labelled separately from recommendations. No production code was changed.*

## Mira lens

Mira's own [global product blueprint](MIRA_GLOBAL_PRODUCT_BLUEPRINT.md) defines a companion for movement through the physical world: know what is evidenced before going, let chosen people follow an active journey, get immediate actions when something feels wrong, then leave one small factual observation that helps the next person. The [contributions specification](docs/CONTRIBUTIONS.md) already distinguishes a contextual MIRA Check, a correction, a lighting vote, and a private incident report. Contributions require independent corroboration and give impact credit only when verified. Mira is currently a Next.js 16 / React 19 mobile web product with MapLibre GL JS, not a React Native app ([package.json](package.json)). These facts control what is transferable.

The market is useful because each product makes a different object primary: an incident, a score, a safety button, a person, a local conversation, a trip, or a small mapping task. Mira's primary object should be **a person's current decision and the relevant, sourced context around it**, with the community visibly improving that context.

It is not needed for factual, independently corroborated micro-contributions. |

### What the cross-product comparison says

1. **Home hierarchy follows a declared job.** Citizen opens the question “what happened?”, Life360 “where are my people?”, Noonlight “can I get help?” and Sitata “what affects my trip?” Mira should answer “what is useful for my next movement, and what has this community actually established here?” This is a product inference from those documented positioning and feature hierarchies, not a copied screen.
2. **The map earns its space only for spatial decisions.** Safetipin, Life360 and Citizen make location highly visible for valid reasons, but their dominant objects are respectively a score/pin, a person and an incident. Mira should let place or journey context lead, opening the map to compare paths, orient to Help Points or inspect precise location.
3. **Community presence needs a truthful object.** “Someone checked these hours” or “two independent people confirmed the entrance” can be tangible without usernames, a public report feed or identity exposure. Mira's current receipts and verification rules can support this only after corroboration; pending input must remain labelled pending.
4. **Alerts need a higher bar than feed content.** Citizen's incident selection and Nextdoor's elevated official alerts show that pushing everything locally relevant is a poor notification rule. Mira should notify for a change that affects an active journey, a chosen place/trip, a requested check-in, or a genuinely urgent verified item. This is a proposed rule, not an observed Mira capability.
5. **Good empty states preserve uncertainty.** GeoSure's “insufficient data” is safer than a reassuring blank score. Mira should show sourced baseline facts, known gaps, Help Points with provenance, and a modest optional check. Never equate silence with safety.

For Mira, the positive pattern is a **reassuring utility loop**: check a place or a planned journey, move with chosen support, arrive, optionally answer one grounded question, later see what the community could confirm. This is a design inference from Mira's own thesis and the comparison above. It does not require a daily hazard feed, artificial streak or public ranking.

### Technical conclusion

The best near-term “reuse” is Mira's own existing code: MapLibre GL JS already renders spatial context; its contribution pipeline already has independent corroboration, contradiction and expiry; trips and trusted-contact links already work locally. The external projects sharpen the interaction model and evidence lifecycle. None justifies adding a dependency for the redesign prototype or changing the production stack.

## Product implications to carry into the redesign

- **Show the result of collective work, not contribution volume.** A community line should state what has been confirmed, by which kind of source and how recently. Avoid a crowd count that users could misread as a safety guarantee.
- **Separate four evidence states.** “From a listed provider,” “confirmed by independent local checks,” “community inputs differ,” and “not enough recent information” require different language and visual treatment. A report awaiting moderation is not a public warning.
- **Give each safety surface a different role.** Home or local context answers the user's question; a journey coordinates chosen people; the map orients; an emergency surface enables immediate action; a private report captures an event. Mixing all five into map pins obscures the product.
- **Make an answer easy to decline.** StreetComplete's one-question focus is valuable, but safety observations can be sensitive. “Didn't notice,” skip and correction should be first-class. Ask after a relevant journey or at a genuinely relevant place, not as a background duty.
- **Design for thin data from day one.** Global reach means many places start with little Mira evidence. Present official and mapped facts with source and age, state gaps plainly, offer help/contact tools, and invite one bounded observation when eligible. Do not generate a numeric “safety” score to fill the blank.
- **Use positive community feedback honestly.** “Your check helped confirm this place's hours” is meaningful when the corroboration gate passed. “You helped keep everyone safe” would overclaim. Mira's existing [contributions specification](docs/CONTRIBUTIONS.md) already requires this distinction.

## Evidence limitations

Public sites and repository documentation do not expose every signed-in first-open screen or A/B variant. Several competitor services vary by region, subscription, device and date. The prototype should be checked with real mobile users; these sources support principles and guardrails, not assumptions about competitor conversion metrics or a claim that their exact layout performs better. Maintenance observations are a snapshot as of 30 September 2026.
